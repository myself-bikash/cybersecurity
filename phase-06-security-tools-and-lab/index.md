# Phase 6: Security Tools and Practical Laboratory

## Phase objective

Learn what common cybersecurity tools are for, how to interpret their output, how to build a safe
virtual lab, and how defensive assessment, forensics, and monitoring activities fit together.

- [ ] Understand the lab boundary and the role of each tool category.

- [ ] Practice with synthetic data, your own device, or isolated intentionally vulnerable systems.
- [ ] Complete the laboratory project report and final assessment.

## Lab safety

Use only systems you own or have explicit authorization to assess. Keep vulnerable virtual machines
on an isolated virtual network, take snapshots, avoid real credentials or confidential data, and
restore the lab after experiments. Do not scan public IP addresses or attempt logins against real
accounts.

To keep the course safe, active scanning, fuzzing, exploitation, credential-recovery, and network
login procedures from the source are covered conceptually rather than as runnable instructions.
Exercises use sample results, synthetic data, or isolated local training applications.

## Source notes

All ten source modules, the lab project, tool definitions, comparison prompts, and 30-question
assessment are mapped in this phase. The PDF includes webpage-title/domain fragments but no
complete bibliography; these fragments are not treated as verified citations.

## Competency checkpoint

Choose one tool for a defined lab question, explain its purpose and limits, and interpret a
synthetic or authorized result. Record scope, evidence, and the next safe step; do not treat a tool
output as a conclusion by itself.
