# Cybersecurity and Information Security

## Learning objectives

By the end of this lesson, you should be able to:

- Explain what cybersecurity and information security mean.
- Distinguish cybersecurity from ethical hacking.
- Describe why cybersecurity matters.
- Distinguish white-hat, black-hat, and grey-hat hackers.

## What is cybersecurity?

Cybersecurity is the practice of protecting networks, devices, applications, and data from
unauthorized access, digital attacks, or damage. Its goal is to keep systems functioning correctly
and ensure data remains secure.

## What is information security?

Information security (InfoSec) is broader than cybersecurity. Cybersecurity focuses on protecting
digital assets from cyber threats, while InfoSec protects information in all forms, including
physical files, conversations, and digital data. The source describes InfoSec in terms of the CIA
triad: confidentiality, integrity, and availability.

## Cybersecurity and ethical hacking

| Cybersecurity | Ethical hacking |
| --- | --- |
| The broader field, including defensive operations and authorized security testing. | A subset of cybersecurity in which professionals are authorized to test systems to find weaknesses so they can be fixed. |

Authorization is essential: testing a system without permission is not ethical hacking.

## Why cybersecurity matters

Personal banking, critical infrastructure, hospitals, and other services depend on digital systems.
A breach can cause financial loss, identity theft, business disruption, or physical harm.
Cybersecurity helps protect privacy, business continuity, and trust.

## Types of hackers

| Type | Description in the source |
| --- | --- |
| White hat | Has explicit permission to test systems and find vulnerabilities to fix. |
| Black hat | Breaks into systems without permission for financial gain, espionage, or destruction. |
| Grey hat | Acts without permission, usually without malicious intent; the source notes that a grey hat may offer to fix a discovered flaw for a fee or bounty. |

Intent does not replace authorization. Do not access or test systems without explicit permission.

## Practical example

Consider a hospital that relies on digital systems. Protecting the systems and data from unauthorized
access is cybersecurity. Protecting related information in digital records, printed files, and
conversations is information security.

## Practical exercise: identify what needs protection

Using a fictional organization or a system you are authorized to assess:

1. List examples of digital assets, such as devices, applications, networks, and data.
2. List examples of information in other forms, such as printed files or conversations.
3. For each item, mark whether it is in scope for cybersecurity, broader information security, or
   both.
4. Do not inspect real accounts, networks, or records unless you have explicit authorization.

## Key takeaways

- Cybersecurity protects digital systems and assets from cyber threats.
- Information security covers information in digital and non-digital forms.
- Ethical hacking is authorized security testing within the broader cybersecurity field.
- Authorization is required before testing a system.
